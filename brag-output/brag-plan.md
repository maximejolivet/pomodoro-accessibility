## The 9-Question Rubric

### 1. What is the app?

A visual timer for people with TDAH, autism, dyslexia, and other neurodivergent profiles. Instead of reading numbers, users watch a colored disk shrink to see how much time is left—making time concrete instead of abstract.

### 2. What is the funniest or most impressive claim?

"On voit le temps qui reste au lieu de le lire" (You see the time remaining instead of reading it). The insight is that for neurodivergent minds, a visual disk is far more intuitive than a digital clock or countdown—no reading, no calculation, no guessing.

### 3. What is the visual hook?

The timer dial itself: a calm, shrinking colored disk against a soft gradient background. The design is elegant and minimalist—accessible without looking medical or patronizing. The color palette is soft and approachable (dusty blue, cream, warm purples for accents).

### 4. What should be shown from the actual UI?

- The main timer page with the shrinking disk in action
- The modes editor (customizable timer presets)
- The routines feature (sequences of timed steps with icons)
- The stats dashboard (daily focus tracking)
- The widget on the home screen

### 5. What is the shortest satisfying video?

18 seconds. Enough to hook on the problem, show the elegance of the visual solution, reveal a few key features (modes, routines), and land the promise: accessible to everyone.

### 6. What tone fits best?

**Tone preset:** `polished` — serious, elegant, premium product feel.  
**Creative direction:** "Quiet premium product film for accessible design." This is not a joke product. It's a carefully designed tool that solves a real problem with elegance. The narration should be thoughtful and human, not corporate.

### 7. What should the audio feel like?

- **Music:** Warm, calm instrumental bed. Something like a subtle lo-fi or modern classical vibe—accessible and inviting without being precious. No upbeat energy; the product speaks quietly.
- **Narration:** Clear, conversational voice. Slightly slower pace, human delivery. The voice complements the visuals, doesn't repeat them.
- **SFX:** Minimal. Consider one subtle tone-based sound when the timer disk appears or at a key reveal, but silence and music should dominate. Possibly the gentle "palier" tone from the app itself as an easter egg.

### 8. What should the share caption say?

"A visual timer for neurodivergent minds. See the time remaining instead of reading it. Available on iOS, Android, and web. Designed for TDAH, autism, dyslexia, and everyone else."

### 9. What's the user flow worth showing?

Not a typical app flow—instead, the visual transformation:
- **Beat 1:** The problem: time is invisible and abstract for neurodivergent brains.
- **Beat 2:** The solution: a shrinking disk makes time visible and concrete.
- **Beat 3:** The depth: modes, routines, quiet alerts, accessibility features.
- **Beat 4:** The promise: elegant, humble, designed with care.

## Storyboard (18 seconds)

### Scene 1: Hook – The Problem (0–2.5s)

**Visual:** Text on a soft gradient background: "Time doesn't feel like time."  
**Voice:** "For many neurodivergent minds, an hour and ten minutes feel the same. Numbers don't help. Neither does watching the clock."  
**Audio:** Music bed fades in—calm, thoughtful.  
**SFX:** None.  
**Duration:** 2.5s  
**Transition:** Fade to next scene.

---

### Scene 2: Reveal – The Solution (2.5–5.5s)

**Visual:** The timer page appears on a smartphone, showing the main timer dial in light theme. The colored disk is full. A hand/finger appears and starts the timer. The disk begins to shrink.  
**Voice:** "Pomodoro Accessibilité shows you the time remaining as it shrinks. One glance—no reading, no math. Just time you can see."  
**Audio:** Music continues. Subtle tone when the timer starts (optional, very soft).  
**SFX:** One gentle chime or tone as the disk appears (brief, ~0.3s).  
**Duration:** 3s  
**Transition:** Disk continues shrinking into next scene.

---

### Scene 3: Features – Modes & Routines (5.5–11s)

**Visual:** Three quick vignettes, each 1.5–2s:

1. **Modes (5.5–7.5s):** The modes sheet slides up, showing preset modes (Pomodoro, Break, Long Break, TDAH Focus). User taps one. Color changes on the timer.
   - **Voice:** "Create your own modes. Pomodoro. Breaks. Focus sessions. Each one remembers your choices."
   
2. **Routines (7.5–9.5s):** The routines panel shows a morning routine: Wake up → Breakfast → Teeth → Backpack (each with an icon and duration). The routine plays through in fast-motion.
   - **Voice:** "Chain timed steps together. A morning routine. A training circuit. A Tabata. Routines can repeat and remind you at a set time."
   
3. **Stats (9.5–11s):** The stats page shows a daily focus goal with a progress bar and a small 7-day graph. Numbers visible but not the focus.
   - **Voice:** "Track your focus time. See your streak. Stay accountable to yourself, not a score."

**Audio:** Music steady, building slightly.  
**SFX:** Subtle transitions between vignettes. Very minimal.  
**Duration:** 5.5s  
**Transition:** Pull back from the phone.

---

### Scene 4: Accessibility & Scope (11–16s)

**Visual:** Show the app in dark mode, in landscape (table mode), and on a widget. Quick cuts, 1-2s each.

1. **Dark mode (11–12.5s):** The timer page in dark theme. Text: "Built for accessibility."
2. **Landscape/Table mode (12.5–14s):** The timer in full-screen landscape mode, as if on a desk or classroom table. Text: "For desks, classrooms, teams."
3. **Widget (14–16s):** The home screen showing the Pomodoro widget with the timer disk visible. Text: "Right on your home screen."

**Voice:** "It works everywhere. Light mode, dark mode, landscape, portrait. On your phone, your tablet, the web. As a widget, right on your home screen."  
**Audio:** Music swells slightly.  
**SFX:** None.  
**Duration:** 5s  
**Transition:** Subtle fade.

---

### Scene 5: Outro – The Promise (16–18s)

**Visual:** The main timer page centered on a clean background. Soft focus on the disk. Text overlays slowly appear:  
- "Pomodoro Accessibilité"
- "For neurodivergent minds."
- "Available on iOS, Android, Web."

**Voice:** "It's free, open-source, and designed with care. For TDAH, autism, dyslexia. For everyone who needs time made concrete."  
**Audio:** Music resolves, fades gently.  
**SFX:** One final, very subtle chime at the very end (0.2s, optional).  
**Duration:** 2s  
**Transition:** Fade to black.

## Audio Guidance

### Music

- **Genre:** Minimalist instrumental, warm ambient, or light lo-fi. Think: Nils Frahm, Jon Hopkins, or similar.
- **Mood:** Calm, approachable, slightly introspective. Not upbeat. Not sad.
- **Cues:**
  - **0–2.5s (Hook):** Music enters softly, establishing the tone.
  - **2.5–5.5s (Reveal):** Music steady, slightly warmer as the solution appears.
  - **5.5–11s (Features):** Gentle progression, builds very slightly as features accumulate.
  - **11–16s (Scope):** Music holds steady, perhaps a subtle swell.
  - **16–18s (Outro):** Music resolves and fades gently to silence.
- **Levels:** -12dB during narration; -6dB when narration ends.

### Narration (Voice with Kokoro)

- **Pace:** Conversational, slightly slower than natural speech. Clear enunciation.
- **Tone:** Warm, human, slightly introspective. Not sales-y, not clinical.
- **Delivery:** Read as if you're talking to a friend who struggles with time. Acknowledge the problem genuinely, then reveal the solution with quiet confidence.

## Notes for Composition

- **Color Palette:** Use the app's actual colors: dusty blue (#a6ccd6), cream/off-white (#fbfbf8), dark ink (#2f3336), and soft purple (#8b6fd6) for accents.
- **Typography:** Sans-serif (Avenir Next or similar). Headings large and breathing; body text readable at normal pace.
- **No generic SaaS language:** Use the app's actual copy: "modes," "routines," "focus time." Avoid "streamline," "optimize," "empower."
- **Show real UI:** Every scene except the hook should show actual screenshots or screen recordings from the app. The UI is the strongest asset.
- **Rhythm:** Cuts should feel unhurried. Let scenes breathe. No frenetic pacing.
- **Accessibility in the video itself:** Captions for all spoken text. High contrast text overlays.
